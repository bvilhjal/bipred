\subsection*{This analysis}

Three cleaning stages are fitted, and their contrast is the result:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.3em}
\item \textbf{harmonised} --- rsID match to the LD reference with allele
  matching and sign flipping only. 924{,}254 variants.
\item \textbf{per-variant QC} --- adds MAF $\geq 0.01$, imputation
  INFO $\geq 0.9$, $\chi^2 \leq 80$, and allele-frequency discordance against
  the reference $\leq 0.20$. 887{,}361 variants.
\item \textbf{+ LD-consistency screen} --- adds the screen of the previous
  section at four rounds. 845{,}623 variants (845{,}423 on the second run).
\end{enumerate}

Each stage is a full joint fit at 200 burn-in and 300 retained sweeps, with
\texttt{cross\_corr} taken from that stage's own cross-trait LDSC intercept.
Long-range LD regions are retained; the MHC is removed with the stage-2
filters.

\subsection*{The chi-square cap applies here too}

The $\chi^2 \leq 80$ term enters at stage 2 and carries into stage 3, so two of
the three rows are fitted on a capped variant set. The cap is inherited from LD
Score regression, whose linear model gives an uncapped large-effect variant
near-full leverage on the slope; the bivariate fit does not need it and gets it
anyway, because both estimators are handed one variant set.

For this pair the cost is genuinely small, and measured rather than assumed.
Over the LD-reference variants, the cap removes 7.6\% of LDL's summed
chi-square (420 variants, largest $\chi^2 = 2{,}013$ at rs445925) and 0.43\% of
CAD's (29 variants, largest $\chi^2 = 443$ at rs2891168, the 9p21 signal). Both
traits are polygenic enough that no single locus carries much of the total.

That is not general. The companion \texttt{oligogenic-overlap} study runs the
same audit across fourteen traits, where the losses reach 73\% for total
bilirubin and 51\% for lipoprotein(a). LDL and CAD are near the bottom of that
range, which is why this study was a poor place to notice the filter at all.

The cap also did not repair the divergence: cancellation for LDL is $255$
before it and $271$ after. Whatever it does at stage 2, it is not what makes
the fit admissible --- that is the screen. \texttt{results/estimates.csv}
carries a third run with \texttt{--chi2-cap regression} that measures the difference
directly; see \emph{What the chi-square cap does here} above.

The generating script is \texttt{benchmarks/real\_ldl\_cad.py}, which stays in
\texttt{benchmarks/} because its other role --- does a real-shaped GWAS still
produce an admissible fit? --- is a regression canary that should be regenerated
freely. What belongs here is the estimate and its reading. The two roles want
opposite handling, which is why they live in different places.

Inputs are not committed; \texttt{benchmarks/README.md} Table 4 records how to
acquire them, and \texttt{../\_lib/traits.toml} registers their column layouts.
