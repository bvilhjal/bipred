\section*{Caveats}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.35em}

\item \textbf{The overlap statistics carry no interval.} The null band is
descriptive, not a test, so ``$+0.3644$ is outside it'' is an observation about
eleven fits and not an inference. This is the largest gap in the analysis.

\item \textbf{\texttt{rg} $= +0.0481$ for Lp(a) $\times$ CAD is not a null
result}, and will be read as one if it is quoted without the architecture that
explains it.

\item \textbf{Every oligogenic reading here is Lp(a).} Its \texttt{n\_causal}
reproduces across three pairings (106, 131, 172), which is reassuring about the
estimate but says nothing about whether the effect generalises to other
oligogenic traits.

\item \textbf{Lp(a)'s \texttt{h2} of $0.007$ is a reference limitation.}
HapMap3 tags the \emph{LPA} KIV-2 repeat poorly. It is not an estimate of Lp(a)
heritability, and the shared-component correlation is measured on what the
reference can see.

\item \textbf{Lp(a) is a four-cohort meta-analysis at 95.9\% European} fitted
against a European LD reference. The European-only alternative covered 16\% of
the reference and was unusable.

\item \textbf{Sample overlap is substantial} --- most exposures are UK Biobank.
The cross-trait LDSC intercept absorbs it and is recorded per pair, but an
intercept cannot identify overlap on its own.

\item \textbf{The Sinnott-Armstrong panel publishes no allele frequency}, so
the AF-concordance check is vacuous for those traits and the $+1.0000$ it
reports is an artifact of comparing a column with itself.

\end{itemize}
