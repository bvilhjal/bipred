\section*{frac\_shared is not the informative statistic}

A second result fell out of the pairs included only to fix the null: sharing
causal \emph{variants} is nearly free, and carries little information on its
own.

SHBG $\times$ ALP was chosen as an unrelated pair of polygenic traits. It shares
61\% of the smaller causal set, and its shared effects correlate at $-0.033$.
Sitting just above it on the same axis, cystatinC $\times$ CRP shares 69\%, gout
$\times$ CRP 71\% and ALP $\times$ GGT 75\% --- and those three correlate at
$+0.316$, $+0.308$ and $+0.283$. For traits that are each causal at thousands to
tens of thousands of variants, substantial overlap of the causal \emph{sets} is
close to the default, and it is the \emph{direction} of the shared effects that
discriminates.

The qualification matters: over the whole range \texttt{frac\_shared} is not
uninformative --- it runs from $0.23$ at the bottom of the scale to $0.99$ at
the top. What it cannot do is separate pairs in the middle, which is where most
real pairs fall.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/frac-shared.pdf}
\caption{\texttt{frac\_shared} against \texttt{rho\_beta}. Across the full
range the two are plainly associated --- it is the middle that cannot be
resolved. Inside the shaded column, four pairs share 61--75\% of their causal
sets while their \texttt{rho\_beta} spans $-0.03$ to $+0.32$. Lp(a) $\times$
CAD sits low on the horizontal axis because Lp(a) has
few causal variants at all, which is the point: a small shared set with a
consistent direction. The horizontal band marks where the three below-zero
pairs fall; it is descriptive, not a test.}
\end{figure}

Lp(a) $\times$ CAD makes the same argument from the other side. It shares 55 of
Lp(a)'s 96 causal variants --- a \texttt{frac\_shared} of $0.570$, lower than
either unrelated polygenic pair --- while its shared effects correlate at
$+0.5170$, higher than any pair that is not the same biology measured twice.
Counting shared variants ranks it below SHBG $\times$ ALP; reading their
direction ranks it fourth of ten.

This is how the overlap summary should be read. \texttt{n\_shared} and
\texttt{frac\_shared} describe how much of the architecture is common;
\texttt{rho\_beta} describes whether the common part agrees. Only the second
distinguishes the pairs here.
