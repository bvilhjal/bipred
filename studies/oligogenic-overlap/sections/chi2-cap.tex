\section*{The chi-square cap}

The per-variant QC caps chi-square at 80. That single filter turned out to
matter more for this study than any other, and it was found by noticing that
Lp(a)'s \texttt{h2} was too small even for a tagging problem: $0.007$, against a
trait whose common \emph{LPA} variants explain a quarter of its variance in
European cohorts.

\subsection*{Where the cap comes from}

It is inherited from LD Score regression, and \texttt{bipred.ldsc.ldsc\_rg} says
so directly: it is a one-step unfiltered LDSC whose regression weights are built
from the fitted means, so a variant whose observed chi-square far exceeds the
LDSC line keeps near-full leverage on the slope. The docstring instructs the
caller to cap extreme chi-square before calling it, and the reference
implementation excludes $\chi^2 > \max(0.001N, 80)$ for the same reason.

The cause is statistical, not numerical. At $N = 284{,}044$ a $\chi^2$ of
$90{,}550$ is a standardised $\hat\beta$ of about $0.56$, well inside the
$|\hat\beta| < 1$ the standardisation requires and nowhere near a precision
limit.

But both drivers built \emph{one} per-variant mask and passed the same variant
set to LD Score regression and to the bivariate fit. So a filter that exists to
protect a linear regression also removed those variants from a mixture model
whose slab component exists to hold large effects. In
\texttt{qc\_factorial.py} it reached further still: \texttt{per\_variant()}
runs upstream of the LD-consistency screen, so the screen never evaluated the
variants the cap had removed.

could not have surfaced this. Those two traits are nearly the least affected of
the fourteen.

\subsection*{What happens when it comes off the fit}

Every pair was refitted with \texttt{--chi2-cap regression}: the cap stays on
the LD Score regression, which needs it, and the joint fit sees every variant.
Both sets of rows are in \texttt{results/estimates.csv} under
\texttt{fit\_chi2\_cap}.

\textbf{Lp(a)'s heritability was suppressed by a factor of six.} It rises from
$0.0069$ to $0.0451$, and the same jump appears in all three of its pairings
($0.0069$, $0.0075$, $0.0078$ becoming $0.0451$, $0.0450$, $0.0448$). Against an
iterate spread of $0.0009$ that is not a wobble. Its \texttt{n\_causal} stays at
90--96, so the recovered heritability went back into the same small set as
larger effects, which is the architecture the cap was flattening. Cancellation
\emph{improved}, $1.69$ to $1.30$: returning the large effects made the fit more
stable, not less.

\textbf{Polygenicity was inflated for every trait.} This is the clearest result
of the rerun, and it has a mechanism: capped, the mixture must account for the
same heritability using only small effects, and the sole way to do that is to
use more of them.

Lp(a) is the exception only because it had nowhere to fall: already at
$\sim$100 causal variants, it absorbed the returned signal as effect size
instead, which is why its \texttt{h2} moved by a factor of six while its count
barely moved at all.

\subsection*{And one fit that the cap was holding together}

\textbf{GGT $\times$ bilirubin diverges without the cap.} Under the cap it was
clean, cancellation $0.37$ and $0.34$. Without it, bilirubin's side reaches
cancellation $177.8$, its \texttt{h2} inflates to $0.488$ and its
\texttt{n\_causal} collapses to 544 --- the signature of a few enormous effects
cancelling through LD rather than explaining variance. rs887829 at \emph{UGT1A1}
carries $\chi^2 = 107{,}799$, and the HapMap3 LD structure cannot support an
effect that large.

So the cap is not simply a bug to be removed. For Lp(a) it was destroying real
signal; for bilirubin it was holding together a fit that cannot otherwise be
made. The redeeming part is that the failure was loud: the divergence
diagnostics caught it immediately rather than returning a plausible wrong
number, which is exactly the case they were calibrated for.

\subsection*{Two claims this rerun overturned}

Both were written earlier in this study and are recorded here rather than
quietly deleted.

\textbf{That the cap inflates \texttt{n\_causal} was reported as unsupported.}
The cross-trait comparison did look against it: the three worst-affected traits
spanned the full range of polygenicity, and the two highest estimates lost less
than the median. That comparison was confounded by traits differing in true
architecture, and the within-trait test --- refit the same trait and watch ---
supports the mechanism decisively, as the table above shows.

\textbf{That \texttt{rho\_beta} moves only where architecture is concentrated
was also wrong.} The largest mover is urate $\times$ cystatinC at $-0.267$,
ahead of Lp(a) $\times$ CAD at $+0.153$. What the eleven pairs actually show is
duller: pairs at the ceiling cannot move, pairs in the null band move by less
than $0.03$, and the mid-range pairs move most, in both directions. Chi-square
loss does not predict it --- dbilirubin $\times$ bilirubin loses about 70\% on
both traits and moves $+0.005$.

The ordering, which is what the study rests on, is nearly unchanged: ten of
eleven pairs stay within one rank, both calibration anchors stay above $+0.98$,
and the null band keeps its membership.
