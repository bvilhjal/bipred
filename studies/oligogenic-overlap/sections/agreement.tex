\section*{Agreement with LD Score regression}

Both reports lean on cross-trait LDSC as the independent check on the joint
fit, so it is worth showing what that check actually looks like across every
pair rather than quoting it one pair at a time.

Three further pairs are drawn in from \texttt{benchmarks/qc\_factorial.csv} at
the same arm 001 --- LDL $\times$ CAD, height $\times$ LDL and HDL $\times$ TG.
They extend the range in both directions: HDL $\times$ TG is the only strongly
negative \texttt{rg} available, and height is the most heritable trait in
either set. They are marked with triangles because they come from a different
script and a separately screened variant set; the factorial's LDL $\times$ CAD
retains 832{,}550 variants where the \texttt{ldl-cad} study retains 845{,}422,
so the two are close but not the same fit.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/rg-agreement.pdf}
\caption{bipred \texttt{rg} against cross-trait LDSC \texttt{rg}. The two agree
on sign and ordering across the full range, from $-0.66$ to $+1.01$. They do
not agree on magnitude: bipred is consistently pulled toward zero at both ends
--- $+0.65$ against LDSC's $+1.01$ for urate $\times$ gout, $-0.50$ against
$-0.66$ for HDL $\times$ TG. Lp(a) $\times$ CAD is the pair where they disagree
in sign, and it is also the pair with the widest LDSC interval: the jackknife
bar runs from $-0.34$ to $+0.15$, so LDSC is not resolving this pair at all.}
\end{figure}

That last point is worth separating from the study's main claim. The reason to
prefer \texttt{rho\_beta} for Lp(a) $\times$ CAD is not that LDSC gives a
different answer. LDSC gives an answer whose interval is five times the width
of the estimate, which is what a genome-wide average does on a trait with
$\sim$100 causal variants.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/h2-agreement.pdf}
\caption{Per-trait \texttt{h2}, both estimators, log scale. Points are one
trait in one pairing, joined where a trait appears more than once; the joins
are therefore an empirical stability check, and most are short. bipred runs
above LDSC nearly everywhere, by roughly a third through the middle of the
range. Two traits sit off that line in opposite directions: CAD, where bipred
is half of LDSC ($0.131 \rightarrow 0.069$), and Lp(a), where it is ten times
LDSC ($0.004 \rightarrow 0.045$) in all three of its pairings.}
\end{figure}

Lp(a) is the interesting one, and it is a direct consequence of the section
that follows. LD Score regression still has the chi-square cap applied to its
rows, because its linear model needs it; the joint fit does not. So on a trait
whose signal is concentrated in a few very large effects, the two estimators
are no longer looking at the same variants, and they should not be expected to
agree. On every polygenic trait, where the cap removes little, they track each
other closely.

The joins also expose one instability worth recording. Gout moves from
\texttt{h2} $0.149$ in urate $\times$ gout to $0.219$ in gout $\times$ CRP, a
47\% change, while LDSC moves $0.129 \rightarrow 0.146$ over the same two fits.
The variant sets differ between pairs, since the screen masks are intersected
per pair, but that is a 3\% difference in $m$ against a near-50\% difference in
\texttt{h2}. No other trait behaves this way.

\subsection*{What is missing here}

Of the four quantities plotted above, two now carry a spread and two do not:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.3em}
\item \textbf{LDSC \texttt{rg}} --- block-jackknife standard error, recorded
  everywhere. These are the horizontal bars.
\item \textbf{bipred \texttt{rg} and \texttt{h2}} --- an iterate SD is now
  recorded for both, in this study and in the factorial. Note what it measures:
  spread across retained Gibbs iterates, which is Monte-Carlo variability of
  the fit and not sampling error of the GWAS. At around 1--2\% of \texttt{h2}
  it is far too narrow to read as a confidence interval, which is why it is not
  drawn as one.
\item \textbf{LDSC \texttt{h2}} --- no standard error at all.
  \texttt{LDSCRgResult} returns \texttt{h2} as a bare tuple.
\item \textbf{\texttt{rho\_beta}} --- nothing, which is the gap that matters
  most, since it is the statistic the study's conclusion rests on.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{BivariateResult} retains \texttt{genetic\_samples}, the raw
$(\mathrm{gvar}_1, \mathrm{gcov}, \mathrm{gvar}_2)$ quadratics at every
post-burn-in sweep, which is where the recorded \texttt{rg} and \texttt{h2}
spreads come from. A defensible interval needs
\texttt{ldpred3\_auto\_bivariate\_chains} and its split-Rhat rather than one
chain's iterates, and \texttt{rho\_beta} needs an equivalent trace of its own.
